I set out to write *Tea of Four Seasons* after a small revelation about jasmine tea. But the moment I started drafting, my mind slipped into an imposing “four seasons” frame: Longjing in spring, a bowl of *liuyueshuang* in summer, osmanthus oolong in autumn, black tea in winter. From there it seemed only natural to map the seasons onto a life—sweet drinks as a child, caffeine at work, flower teas with age, and finally a return to pure, plain tea.

Halfway through, I realized the frame was fake. I had no deep feelings about black tea. At twenty-five I happily drink soda, coffee, flower tea, and plain tea—all at once. If something tastes good, I’m in. I don’t write for a living. Why force a “complete” structure and write for the sake of writing?

<!-- more -->

This obsession with completeness, I realized, is inertia left over from school—and it had already leaked into my work.

Take note-taking. I used to want to start from the creation of the world and spin out an entire system. But I don’t sell notes or courses. What I actually need is notes that are self-consistent within a defined scope. Park the grand knowledge map somewhere else. Spending hours polishing the twigs of a framework is putting the cart before the horse.

At university we kept the high-school habit of chasing “big and complete.” In the AI era that path no longer works. It manufactures the false comfort of “I took so many notes and spent so many hours.” We mistake time invested for knowledge absorbed, and forget the efficiency of absorption and conversion. Chasing tidy form and total coverage is, at bottom, **using tactical diligence to hide strategic laziness**—self-consumption without meaning.

Large language models can already spare us oceans of filtering. Faced with new tools, we have to redefine our core value: build the macro architecture of knowledge, verify what the model returns, and delegate the micro-filling to AI. In this era, stepping out of low-value mechanical labor and concentrating on judgment, structure, and thought—that is what is worth doing.
